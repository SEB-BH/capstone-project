# ![Capstone Project Deliverables](./assets/hero.png)

## Overview

The Capstone is deliverable-driven. You will submit work throughout the two-week sprint instead of waiting until presentation day.

> ✅ **All six deliverables must be complete to pass the Capstone.**

One submission per team is sufficient for shared deliverables unless your instructor tells you otherwise.

## Deliverable schedule

| Deliverable | Target date | What it proves |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Discovery Brief | Mon, Aug 24 | You understand the problem, user/client, and goals. |
| 2. Project Plan & Scope Approval | Mon, Aug 24 | Your MVP and technical plan are achievable and approved. |
| 3. Midpoint Checkpoint & Retro | Thu, Aug 27 | Your backlog reflects reality and Week 2 is re-scoped appropriately. |
| 4. Testing & Feedback | Tue, Sep 1 | Real users/clients have reviewed the work and you responded thoughtfully. |
| 5. Final Project & Handoff/Launch | Wed, Sep 2 | The product is ready to deliver and documented. |
| 6. Capstone Presentation | Thu, Sep 3 | You can professionally communicate the problem, process, and product. |

Your instructor may adjust exact submission times during project week.

## 1. Discovery Brief

### Client Track

Complete the [Client Project Brief](../templates/client-project-brief.md) using information from the client kickoff meeting and any supplied client materials.

Your brief should identify:

- Client and point of contact.
- Business/project context.
- Target users.
- Problem and pain points.
- Desired outcomes and success criteria.
- Must-have requirements and nice-to-have requests.
- Constraints, assets, existing systems, and dependencies.
- Open questions and risks.
- Initial proposed solution.

### Startup Track

Complete the [Startup Discovery Brief](../templates/startup-discovery-brief.md).

Your brief should identify:

- Working startup name.
- Problem statement.
- Target customer/user.
- Pain points.
- Proposed solution.
- Initial competitor/market research.
- Assumptions that need validation.
- Initial success criteria.

## 2. Project Plan & Scope Approval

Submit a publicly visible or instructor-accessible Trello board containing the planning materials appropriate to your project.

### Required for all projects

- **MVP user stories** using the format: *As a [user], I want [feature], so that [reason].*
- **Acceptance criteria** on each MVP user story.
- **Sprint backlog** showing the work required to deliver the MVP.
- **Wireframes** for the important user-facing screens or experiences.
- **MVP definition** describing what will be delivered by the end of the Capstone.
- **Stretch goals** clearly separated from MVP work.
- **Team responsibilities/ownership** where working in a group.
- **Technical approach** describing the major technologies, services, and architecture you plan to use.

### When applicable

Include technical planning artifacts that meaningfully support the project, such as:

- ERD or data model.
- Component hierarchy diagram.
- API/route plan.
- Authentication/authorization flow.
- Third-party API or service research.
- Integration plan.
- Deployment plan.
- Other diagrams or documentation useful to your solution.

> Your proposal must be approved by the instructional team before substantial feature development begins.

## Using Trello during the Capstone

Trello is not only a planning artifact for this project. **Update your sprint backlog every project day.**

Your board should make it easy to see:

- What is not started.
- What is currently in progress.
- What is blocked.
- What is complete.
- What has changed since the initial plan.

## 3. Midpoint Checkpoint & Retro

By the midpoint checkpoint:

- Trello accurately reflects current progress.
- Completed stories/tasks are moved and acceptance criteria are updated.
- GitHub shows meaningful development activity.
- The current product/build can be reviewed where applicable.
- Blockers and risks are documented.
- Remaining MVP work has been re-estimated or re-prioritized.
- Stretch goals have been reconsidered.
- The team completes the [Midpoint Retro](../templates/midpoint-retro.md).

An informal demo is encouraged when it is useful, but a polished Week 1 demo is not required.

## 4. Testing & Feedback

Complete the [Testing & Feedback Log](../templates/testing-feedback.md).

Every project must be reviewed or tested by someone outside the development team.

### Client Track

The reviewer should normally be the client, stakeholder, or intended user identified with the client.

### Startup Track

Use potential users or customers. The provisional minimum is:

- **5 meaningful survey responses**, or
- **3 short user/customer conversations**, or
- another validation method approved by your instructor.

Document:

- Who provided feedback.
- What was tested or reviewed.
- What feedback was received.
- What changed because of feedback.
- What you intentionally chose not to implement and why.
- Any unresolved usability or technical issues discovered during testing.

## 5. Final Project & Handoff/Launch

### Required for all projects

- Completed approved MVP, or a documented/re-approved revision.
- Deployed/published product where appropriate.
- Final GitHub repository/repositories.
- Current Trello board.
- README/project documentation.
- Known issues and limitations.
- Future enhancements or recommended next steps.

### Client Track

Complete the [Client Handoff Checklist](../templates/client-handoff.md) and provide the client with the information and access needed to continue using or maintaining the project.

The handoff package should include deployment links, repository links, setup instructions, account/service information, known issues, maintenance considerations, and next steps.

**Never put passwords, tokens, API keys, or other secrets inside the README, Trello board, or handoff document.** Transfer credentials separately using an appropriate method.

### Startup Track

Provide evidence that the startup has moved beyond an idea and has been prepared for launch. This normally includes:

- Working/deployed MVP where appropriate.
- Startup name and visual identity.
- Clear description of the target customer and value proposition.
- Evidence of market/user validation.
- Launch, acquisition, or awareness materials appropriate to the startup.
- Clear next steps after Capstone.

## 6. Capstone Presentation

Prepare a slide deck and live product demo for presentation day.

- **Presentation:** approximately 8-10 minutes.
- **Questions:** approximately 2-3 minutes.
- Every team member participates.
- Every team member demonstrates or discusses work they meaningfully contributed to.

Recommended presentation story:

1. Project/startup introduction.
2. Problem and target user/client.
3. Discovery and research.
4. Proposed solution and MVP.
5. Planning/development process.
6. Live demo.
7. Testing, feedback, and iteration.
8. Technical highlights.
9. Challenges and lessons learned.
10. Next steps.

### Branding requirements

- **Client Track:** opening and closing slides show the bootcamp logo and client logo.
- **Startup Track:** opening and closing slides show the bootcamp logo and startup logo.

See the [Presentation Checklist](../templates/presentation-checklist.md).
