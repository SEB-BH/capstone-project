## Capstone mindset

The goal of the Capstone is not to build the largest possible application. The goal is to practice working like a developer and deliver something real.

A focused MVP that solves the right problem and works well is more valuable than an over-scoped project filled with unfinished features.

## Working on a team

- Share ideas and concerns early.
- Listen to and respect teammates.
- Make responsibilities visible on the backlog.
- Ask for help when blocked instead of silently losing time.
- Review one another's work and communicate before making major changes.
- Keep the team aligned on the approved MVP.

### Git collaboration

Use a Git workflow appropriate to your project. For shared repositories, coordinate branches, pull requests, and merges so that work is visible and conflicts are manageable.

## Daily project rhythm

Each project day begins with a short standup:

- **Yesterday:** What did you complete?
- **Today:** What are you planning to complete?
- **Blockers:** What is preventing progress?

Standups are a project-week expectation, but attendance is tracked separately from the Capstone rubric.

Update the Trello sprint backlog every day so it reflects reality. Move completed work, revise cards, add newly discovered tasks, and identify blockers.

## Scope management

### Before approval

You may research, explore a client codebase, configure tools, or build small prototypes. Avoid substantial feature development until the instructional team approves the project plan and MVP.

### After approval

Expect the plan to change. When a new client request, user request, or technical discovery appears:

1. Document it.
2. Estimate its impact on the existing MVP and timeline.
3. Decide what would need to move, change, or be removed.
4. Review significant scope changes with the instructional team.

Do not silently expand the project because someone requested another feature.

## Project assistance

The instructional team will hold project check-ins and individual one-on-ones throughout the two weeks. Use them to surface blockers, technical uncertainty, group issues, and scope concerns.

## Presentation guidance

Plan for **8-10 minutes of presentation time** and **2-3 minutes of questions**.

A strong presentation tells a story:

**Problem → Discovery → Solution → Process → Product → Feedback → Lessons → Next Steps**

Your slide deck should support the story rather than contain every detail. The live demo should focus on the most important user flow or outcome.

Prepare a backup demo using screenshots or a short recording in case deployment, internet access, or an external service fails on presentation day.
