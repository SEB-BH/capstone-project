## Overview

In the Client Track, your team will work with a real client to solve a real business or organizational problem.

The goal is not to force every project into the same technology stack. Your job is to understand the client need, recommend an appropriate solution, scope it realistically, build it professionally, collect feedback, and hand it off in a usable state.

## Client kickoff

Use the kickoff meeting to understand the client rather than immediately promising features.

Complete the [Client Project Brief](../templates/client-project-brief.md) after the meeting.

Ask about:

- The problem the client wants solved.
- Who experiences the problem.
- Current process or existing solution.
- What a successful outcome would look like.
- Must-have requirements.
- Nice-to-have ideas.
- Existing assets, branding, systems, or code.
- Technical or business constraints.
- Who can approve decisions and provide feedback.
- What should explicitly be considered out of scope.

## Scope and change requests

The kickoff conversation begins the project scope; it does not create an unlimited list of promises.

After the instructional team approves the MVP, new requests should be added to the backlog and evaluated before being accepted. Significant changes must be discussed with the instructional team.

If a new request is accepted, be prepared to explain what changed in the original plan.

## Feedback

Before final delivery, the client or an appropriate stakeholder should review the work.

Document:

- What they reviewed.
- What they liked or found useful.
- Problems or requests they identified.
- What the team changed.
- What the team chose not to change and why.

## Client handoff

A successful client project is not finished when the code works on a student's laptop.

Prepare a professional handoff containing the information the client needs to access, understand, and continue the project. Use the [Client Handoff Checklist](../templates/client-handoff.md).

Depending on the project, this may include:

- Deployed URLs.
- Repository access or ownership transfer.
- Setup/run instructions.
- Hosting/domain/service information.
- Administrative instructions.
- Known issues and limitations.
- Maintenance considerations.
- Recommended next steps.

Transfer passwords and secret credentials separately. **Never include secrets in public documentation or repositories.**

## Presentation

Your presentation should explain the client problem, discovery process, approved solution, development work, feedback, final result, and next steps.

The opening and closing slides must include:

- Bootcamp logo.
- Client logo.
- Project name.
- Team members.
