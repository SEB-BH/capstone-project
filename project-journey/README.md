# ![Capstone Project Journey](./assets/hero.png)

The Capstone is structured around a professional project workflow rather than a single final deadline. You will have required deliverables throughout the two weeks to help you stay on track.

> ✅ **Every required Capstone deliverable must be complete in order to pass the project.**

## Project info

Before starting, review the [Project Requirements](../project-requirements/README.md), your track guide, and the [Project Details](../project-details/README.md).

If you are working with a team, you will also be evaluated individually. It is possible for a team to pass the project while an individual contributor does not. Review the [Individual Contributor Requirements](../project-individual-contributor-requirements/README.md).

## The Capstone journey

### 1. Discover

Understand the problem, the people you are building for, and what success should look like.

**Required Deliverable 1: Discovery Brief**

- Client Track: complete the Client Project Brief after the kickoff meeting.
- Startup Track: complete the Startup Discovery Brief and initial market research.

### 2. Plan

Turn the discovery work into an achievable MVP and a development plan.

**Required Deliverable 2: Project Plan & Scope Approval**

Your team will create and submit the appropriate planning materials, including a Trello sprint backlog, user stories, acceptance criteria, wireframes, MVP scope, stretch goals, and relevant technical planning artifacts.

You may research technologies, inspect an existing codebase, configure tools, or create small technical prototypes before approval. **Substantial development should not begin until your project scope is approved by the instructional team.**

### 3. Build

Work through your sprint backlog. Keep Trello current throughout the project and use Git/GitHub to show meaningful progress.

Each project day begins with a short standup:

- What did I accomplish yesterday?
- What am I working on today?
- What blockers do I have?

At key points, also ask: **Is our current MVP scope still realistic?**

### 4. Checkpoint

Review progress at the end of Week 1 and make a realistic plan for Week 2.

**Required Deliverable 3: Midpoint Checkpoint & Retro**

A demo is encouraged when useful, but is not required for the midpoint checkpoint.

### 5. Validate

Put the work in front of someone outside the development team, test it, collect feedback, and decide what to change.

**Required Deliverable 4: Testing & Feedback**

Good product development does not mean implementing every request. You should document what feedback you received, what you changed, and what you intentionally chose not to change.

### 6. Deliver

Finish the MVP, deploy or publish it where appropriate, document it, and prepare the appropriate handoff or launch materials.

**Required Deliverable 5: Final Project & Handoff/Launch**

### 7. Showcase

Present the problem, process, product, feedback, technical work, and lessons learned to the class, clients, and guests.

**Required Deliverable 6: Capstone Presentation**

The presentation should be approximately **8-10 minutes**, followed by **2-3 minutes of questions**.
