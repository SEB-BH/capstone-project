## Capstone structure

This Capstone has **two official tracks only**:

1. **Client Track**
2. **Startup Track**

The Startup Track includes an optional student-facing idea starter for launching a service-based software/web business. It should remain presented as an example of a valid Startup concept, not as a separate track.

## Primary outcomes

The Capstone prioritizes:

1. Experience working like a developer.
2. Pride in building something real.
3. A strong portfolio/resume item.

## Timeline

- Sun Aug 23: kickoff, guest speaker, client kickoff meetings, discovery.
- Mon Aug 24: Discovery Brief + Planning & Scope Approval.
- Tue-Wed Aug 25-26: build.
- Thu Aug 27: Midpoint Checkpoint & Retro; optional demo.
- Sun-Mon Aug 30-31: re-scope, build, feedback.
- Tue Sep 1: Testing & Feedback due.
- Wed Sep 2: Final Project & Handoff/Launch due; feature freeze, documentation, rehearsal.
- Thu Sep 3: Capstone presentations.

Treat the two weeks as one Capstone sprint with a formal midpoint checkpoint. This preserves the option to describe the work as two sprints later without changing the core deliverables.

## Daily rhythm

Run a short morning standup:

- Yesterday.
- Today.
- Blockers.

At the midpoint and beginning of Week 2, ask: **Is the current MVP scope still realistic?**

Students should update Trello daily. Standup participation is a project-week expectation, but attendance is tracked separately and should not be scored in the Capstone rubric.

## Scope approval

Students may perform research, environment setup, existing-codebase review, and small technical prototypes before approval. Substantial feature development should wait until the instructional team approves the scope.

Use approval to prevent both over-scoping and under-scoping. There is no universal Capstone technology requirement; technical complexity should make sense for the project.

## Client change requests

After scope approval, students should document new client requests and discuss meaningful scope changes with the instructional team. A request from a client is not automatically required for the Capstone MVP.

This is an opportunity to teach tradeoffs, prioritization, and stakeholder communication.

## Deliverable policy

All six required deliverables must be complete to pass:

1. Discovery Brief.
2. Project Plan & Scope Approval.
3. Midpoint Checkpoint & Retro.
4. Testing & Feedback.
5. Final Project & Handoff/Launch.
6. Capstone Presentation.

One shared submission per team is sufficient for team deliverables. Individuals are evaluated separately using the Individual Contributor rubric.

## Startup validation

Current provisional minimum:

- 5 meaningful survey responses, or
- 3 short user/customer conversations, or
- another instructor-approved validation method.

This number can be adjusted before launch without changing the overall project structure.

## Client brief and handoff

Require each Client team to complete the standardized Client Project Brief after kickoff, even if the client provides separate documentation. This gives the instructional team a consistent scope artifact.

Require a formal handoff package before the presentation. Actual credential transfer may occur after presentation if that timing is more appropriate for the client.

## Presentations

Target:

- 8-10 minutes presenting.
- 2-3 minutes Q&A.

Audience may include the full class, clients, staff, and guests.

Require a slide deck. Opening and closing slides must include:

- Client: bootcamp logo + client logo.
- Startup: bootcamp logo + startup logo.

Every student in a group must participate and discuss/demonstrate work they meaningfully contributed to.

## Evaluation

Use:

- Track-specific project requirements spreadsheet.
- Individual Contributor requirements spreadsheet for group members.

A team may pass while an individual does not pass. Missing required deliverables always require remediation, even if final product quality is otherwise strong.
