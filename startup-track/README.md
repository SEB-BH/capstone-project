## Overview

In the Startup Track, you will identify a real problem or opportunity, research the market, validate assumptions with potential users or customers, build an MVP, and prepare the startup for launch.

A startup is more than an app idea. Your Capstone should show evidence of:

**Problem → Customer → Research → Solution → MVP → Feedback → Launch**

## Startup discovery

Complete the [Startup Discovery Brief](../templates/startup-discovery-brief.md).

Your early work should answer:

- Who is the target customer/user?
- What problem or pain point do they experience?
- How are they solving it today?
- What alternatives or competitors exist?
- Why might your proposed solution be useful or different?
- What assumptions need to be tested before you spend too much time building?

## Validation

Before final submission, collect evidence from potential users or customers.

The provisional minimum is:

- **5 meaningful survey responses**, or
- **3 short user/customer conversations**, or
- another method approved by the instructional team.

Validation is not about proving that your first idea was correct. Useful feedback may lead you to change the product, narrow the audience, or remove a feature.

## Branding and launch

Your startup should have enough identity to communicate clearly to potential users/customers and to present professionally.

At minimum, prepare:

- Startup name.
- Simple visual identity/logo.
- Clear value proposition.
- Launch or awareness materials appropriate to the startup.
- A way for interested users/customers to learn more, try the product, or contact the team where appropriate.

## Startup idea starter: launch a service-based business

If you are struggling to choose a product idea, you may build a startup around a **service you can provide using your software engineering skills**.

For example, you could launch a small web studio focused on a specific market such as restaurants, salons, fitness businesses, tutors, local retailers, or professional services.

A strong version of this idea would include:

### Define the market

- Choose a specific customer niche.
- Research several existing businesses in that niche.
- Identify common website/digital pain points.
- Research competitors offering similar services.

### Build the brand and offering

- Business name and logo/visual identity.
- Clear services and value proposition.
- Service/package information or a simple pricing approach.
- Business/portfolio website.

### Build reusable proof of work

Create **at least two polished reusable templates or demo sites** designed for the chosen market.

The goal is to show that you can deliver useful work repeatedly rather than build only one personal portfolio page.

### Create passive lead-generation opportunities

You are not required to cold-call or aggressively contact strangers. Instead, create several realistic ways that potential clients could discover the business or contact you.

Examples include:

- Contact/lead form on the business website.
- LinkedIn or professional social presence.
- Business social-media account.
- Posts showcasing the templates or services.
- Freelance marketplace profile.
- Niche-specific landing page.
- QR code/business card linking to the portfolio.
- Appropriate community/professional group presence.
- Simple referral messaging or shareable service information.

Also create a small researched list of prospective businesses that fit your target market. You are graded on the quality of the business launch and lead-generation setup, **not on whether someone hires you during Capstone week**.

## Presentation

The opening and closing slides must include:

- Bootcamp logo.
- Startup logo.
- Startup/project name.
- Team members.

Your presentation should explain the market problem, research, validation, solution, MVP, launch approach, feedback, technical work, and next steps.
